# Payroll funding policy

User direction: normal NFL franchise play must not require adjusting revenue
to afford player payments. This is a simulation policy, not a claim that every
real owner has unlimited liquidity.

Default franchise accounting assumes the owner funds normal contractual player
obligations. Gate receipts, attendance, losses, market size and missing revenue
data must not cause artificial payroll shortages or force revenue sliders to
be tuned. Keep owed/paid compensation records accurate; those totals are not
a cash balance or an insolvency test. Never erase unpaid obligations when
funding is assumed.

Salary-cap room, retained/dead charges, guarantees, rookie allocations and
applicable contract rules remain binding. Owner funding cannot increase the
cap, remove debt, change posted salary, bypass a rookie pool, or mark money as
paid without an actual payment record.

Cash structure can still matter in negotiation: security, guaranteed amounts
and payment timing affect player/owner preferences. Financial flexibility means
the team's contract and cap strategy, not routine inability to pay salaries.

Exceptional distress may be modeled only as an explicit severe-mismanagement
scenario with known causes and separate funding/liability state. It is not
enabled by default and must not be inferred by the LLM from a losing season.

Current CashBook tracks obligations and payments, not revenues or a bank balance;
it has no revenue-based payroll gate. Preserve that separation during settlement,
season rollover, game integration and future ownership/business mechanics.
